Tone check

Every joke against the project's rules: jokes land on the persona; walls and Deport only ever hit Spire monsters; sleepiness, confusion and gaffes are fine, age and health are not; nothing about immigrants, real groups, real events or other real people.

Music brief

Five short cues, generated separately with MiniMax Music 3 on your PC, several takes each, then edited together with hits, a record scratch and a tape stop, the way trailer music is usually cut. After the first round you asked for everything deeper, harder and deep-fried: low-tuned, bass-boosted and saturated, with the mixer able to push any take further (slowed tape, lower pitch, bass, drive).

1 · The Spire

0:00–0:10 · D minor

Dark hybrid-orchestral opening: low string drone, distant choir, a slow taiko pulse, a riser into a braam.

2 · The Donald

0:10–0:25.5 · D minor · 140 BPM

Menacing swagger: massive low brass over distorted 808s and hard trap or phonk drums. Bass-boosted, saturated, loud.

3 · Lullaby

0:26–0:36 · slow

A slowed, pitched-down music box drenched in reverb and tape warble, over a deep sub-bass drone. Ends in a tape stop.

4 · Dark Brandon

0:37–0:48 · D minor · 100 BPM

Aggressive 80s synthwave: gated drums, distorted analog bass, arpeggios, with orchestral braams and taiko on top.

5 · Finale

0:48–1:03 · 140 BPM

Relentless and colossal: low brass, a low choir, taiko and distorted 808s or orchestral phonk, building through the collection. Stops dead on one enormous hit under the title.

Sound design

  • Trailer kit: braams, risers, sub drops, whooshes on cuts, impacts on hits.
  • Gags: record scratch, tape stop, the aviator glint, snores, typing clicks.
  • Character sounds: coin showers, the rubber stamp, the cash register, the laser charge.
  • The game's own combat sounds, captured with the footage where possible.

Mix

  • −14 LUFS integrated, true peak −1 dB (YouTube's target).
  • Narration sits on top; music ducks under it.
  • 48 kHz stereo.

Look and feel

  • Name cards: THE DONALD in gold foil; SLEEPY JOE in navy with letters that droop; DARK BRANDON in red neon with a glitch.
  • Camera: the game renders at 4K, so shots can push in up to 1.5× with no loss at 1440p. Slow drifts on calm shots, shakes on hits.
  • Pacing: "holy shit" energy. In the action, a cut every 0.3 to 1 s, on the beat; each beat on the storyboard becomes two or three shots.
  • Deep-fried hits: on the biggest moments (the Big Beautiful Wall, YOU'RE FIRED!, the Dark Brandon drop, the title) a few blown-out frames: crushed contrast, oversaturated color, a zoom punch, a hard shake, a chromatic split, and a bass-boosted hit in the sound.
  • Cuts: whip pans and flash frames on hits, speed ramps (slow motion into an impact, then snapping to double speed).
  • Card callouts: when a signature card is played, its clean render slides in large beside the action, so viewers learn the cards while they watch them work.
  • Frame: full 16:9 for gameplay; letterbox bars only on the painted AI shots, so they read as cinematics.
  • Grade: a warm gold push in The Donald's act, cool indigo for Sleepy Joe, crushed blacks and red for Dark Brandon.
  • Type: the game's own serif family for gameplay text, so the trailer feels like part of the game.
